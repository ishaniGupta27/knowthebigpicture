Evaluate one familiar claim without forcing false certainty.

Use this path:

familiar claim -> short verdict -> what it gets right -> what it gets wrong or
leaves out -> why people believe it -> practical takeaway

The verdict should be one of: true, mostly true, partly true, misleading, mostly
false, false, or depends. Use role `misconception` for the verdict and correction.
Do not shame viewers. Preserve important qualifications and never turn "may" into
"does." The opening slide presents the exact claim or question; the next slide
should give the verdict promptly. End with what the viewer should remember or do.

Use 5-6 slides total, including the opening question slide. The verdict slide is
priority 1.

### Opening hook (narration, question slide)
State the familiar belief, then cast honest doubt (a question, never a false claim).
Openers: "You've probably heard that X. But is it actually true?", "Most people believe X. Here's the truth."
